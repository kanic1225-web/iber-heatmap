## Geometry (70 regions)
- 46 prefectures: `dataofjapan/land` `japan.geojson` (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dataofjapan/land/master/japan.geojson).
- 23 Tokyo wards: `niiyz/JapanCityGeoJson` per-city files `geojson/13/13101.json` to `13123.json`, based on MLIT N03 boundaries. (The combined `13/13.json` returned 404.)
- "Tokyo outside 23 wards": the dataofjapan Tokyo polygon minus the dissolved wards (`mapshaper -erase`). Sliver parts under 0.5 km² were dropped. The two sources don't match exactly along the ward edge, which you can't see at national scale.
- Tokyo remote islands: the Izu islands and Ogasawara are kept. Okinotorishima (20°N) and Minamitorishima (154°E) are dropped so the map can fit Japan. Prefecture islets under 1.5 km² were dropped.
- Simplification: mapshaper 0.7.101 (`npx -y mapshaper`). Prefectures: interval 500 m, 0.001° precision. Tokyo-rest: 300 m. Wards: 60 m, 0.0002° precision. Rings were rewound clockwise for d3. Inlined GEO is about 340 KB, and the whole file is about 406 KB. A whole-Tokyo outline (`JP-13`, interval 300 m) is added for the main map.
- topojson-client 3.1.0 is not on cdnjs (404), so the geometry is inlined as plain GeoJSON and only d3 7.9.0 is loaded.
- Since 2026-11-02 the folder builds on its own (it went public on GitHub that day): the simplified shapes live in `geo/` (see `geo/SOURCES.md`), the scripts in `tools/` (`prep.py`, `gen.py`, `make_dist.py`, `template.html`, `build.sh`). `bash tools/build.sh` from any directory rewrites `index.artifact.html` and `dist/`, and writes its log and optional screenshots to the ignored `build/` folder. The raw downloads (dataofjapan `japan.geojson`, the 23 ward files, the dealer pages) are not kept in the repo; `prep.py` is the script that turned them into the `geo/` files.

## Can pay (income) — REAL, with one estimated piece
- 2023 Housing and Land Survey (令和5年住宅・土地統計調査), 確報集計. Prefectures: 第40-1表 (statInfId 000040209911). Wards: 第43-4表 (statInfId 000040209921). Household concept 主世帯; shares are bracket ÷ households with income stated (不詳 left out). Details in `data/income_sources.md`.
- Each region stores `money: { m10, m15, m20 }` = share of households with income over ¥10,000,000 / ¥15,000,000 / ¥20,000,000. The slider picks one of the three directly; the old `incomeFactor` formula is gone.
- Check shares: Japan 8.15 % / 2.11 % / 0.84 %; all Tokyo 14.45 % / 4.75 % / 2.02 %; the 23 wards together 15.8 % / 5.5 % / 2.41 %. (These run lower than the 国民生活基礎調査 figures people often quote; it is a different survey.)
- Ward gap: the ward table's top bracket is "1500万円以上", in 2023 and in 2018, so no survey gives "over ¥20,000,000" per ward. `m20` for each ward is ESTIMATED: a power-law tail is fitted to the ward's own real m10 and m15 (`alpha = ln(m15/m10) / ln(1000/1500)`, `raw20 = m15 × 0.75^alpha`), then all wards are scaled by one factor k = 0.8894 so their over-¥20m households add up to the real 23-ward total. Results: 港区 11.9 %, 千代田区 8.4 %, 中央区 7.3 %, 渋谷区 3.9 %. Ward regions carry `m20_est: true`, and the tooltip says "estimated" at the ¥20,000,000 stop. Tokyo outside the wards has a real m20 (prefecture table minus the ward block).
- With only "Can pay" on, Japan counts 4,546,274 / 1,177,687 / 471,514 households at the three stops. With all four doors on at ¥20,000,000 the page shows 61,640, but that number still depends on the three placeholder doors.
- On the page, yen amounts are always written in full (never "¥20m") and the word "over" is used instead of "≥". The Percentage scale top is 40 % (central wards pass 10 % on "Can pay" alone at the ¥10m stop).

## Placeholder numbers (still mock)
- Door pass rates `park`, `charge` and `timing` are placeholders. The old combined rate `pk` ("can park and charge": PREF_PARK for the eight urban prefectures, 0.80–0.95 bands elsewhere, 0.15–0.45 per ward, 0.60 for Tokyo outside the wards) and `timing` follow the bands in the brief, with seeded noise (seed 983). On 2026-11-01 `pk` was split by `split_park()` in `gen.py`: `park = 0.55 + 0.45 × pk` (rounded to 4 decimals) and `charge = pk ÷ park` (6 decimals), so `park × charge = pk` exactly and no total moved (all four doors on at ¥20,000,000 still gives 61,640). The split adds no random draws, so the seeded sequence is unchanged (the old mock household and money numbers are still in `gen.py`'s PREF/WARDS tables, marked UNUSED, for that reason). `low` = base × 0.75 and `high` = base × 1.25, clamped to [0,1]; the UI reads `base` only.
- Real-data path for `park`: in Japan a car can only be registered with proof of a parking space (車庫証明), so passenger cars per household by prefecture (自動車検査登録情報協会, yearly; municipality tables exist for the wards) is a good stand-in. `charge` is what Team 3's condo parking audit measures (share of parking spaces that can take a charger), so it stays a placeholder until the fieldwork is done.
- `DATA_STATUS` in the data block records which inputs are real; the page does not read it.

## How the page works (single view)
- Main map: 47 prefecture units. Tokyo (`JP-13`, a whole-Tokyo outline) is the SUM of its 24 pieces: the 23 wards plus Tokyo outside the wards. The inset shows the 23 wards as separate units.
- Value per unit: households passing the checked doors = households × the product of the checked doors' pass rates. In Percentage mode it is that ÷ households × 100. The side panel shows the national total of the same numbers and the Top 8 prefectures (the step-by-step funnel table was removed on 2026-11-01; `afterDoors()` went with it if nothing else used it).
- Color: a three-stop ramp, theme tokens `--ramp0` (white, t = 0), `--ramp-mid` (`#61BCF6`, t = 0.5) and `--ramp1` (`#12256E`, t = 1), blended in plain sRGB so it looks like a CSS `linear-gradient(90deg, #FFFFFF, #61BCF6 50%, #12256E)`.
  - The scale is linear only: t = value ÷ top, with top = 2,000,000 households or 10%. (The Log option was removed on 2026-10-31.)
  - Contrast (default 100) stretches t between the palest and darkest unit, separately for the main map and the inset. At 0 the scale is fixed, so checking another door can only make a region paler.
- No doors checked: the coloured layer fades to `fill-opacity: 0` but KEEPS its last fill. (Fixed 2026-11-01: it used to be repainted with the "households only" colours, which in household mode are navy for most of the map because Tokyo's 7.2 million households sit far above the 2,000,000 scale top. The CSS transition then crossfaded fill and opacity together, so toggling a door from the all-off state blinked dark.) The proof scripts were throwaway headless-Chrome checks and are not in the repo.
- Dealers: dots on both maps (ink with a white halo), toggled by "Show dealers".
- Own numbers (added 2026-11-02, the morning of the interim presentation, after Gem suggested Porsche people should be able to enter their own values): each placeholder door row (park, charge, timing) has a 56 px number box at its right edge (`own-park`, `own-charge`, `own-timing`) showing that door's households-weighted national average over the 70 REGIONS, rounded (87 / 81 / 22 on the current placeholders, computed on load, not hardcoded). Typing V sets an override: every region's rate becomes min(1, base × k) with k found by bisection so the national average equals V%, so the regional pattern is kept and only the level moves. Empty, non-numeric or within 0.5 points of the default = override off. Tooltip tag becomes " · your number"; a Reset button (`own-reset`, hidden until an override is active) sits after the footer; overrides are mirrored in the address as `?park=60&charge=40&timing=20` (only the overridden doors) and read back on load, also from file:// URLs. Nothing is uploaded or stored anywhere else. To fit the boxes on one line the sub-texts became "has parking", "charger OK", "in 24 months". The footer now reads "(Can park, Can charge, Renewal soon are mock data; type your own in the boxes)".
- Contrast info icon (same day): a 14 px circled "i" after the CONTRAST label (`tabindex=0`, `role=button`, `aria-describedby=contrast-tip`). Hover, focus, Enter/Space or a tap opens a 260 px card styled like the map tooltip; mouseleave, blur, Escape or a click elsewhere closes it. Text: "At 0%, colours sit on a fixed scale (0 to 2,000,000 households, or 0 to 40%), so you can compare views with different doors or income cutoffs. At 100%, the palest area on each map turns white and the darkest turns navy: you see the biggest differences, but colours can't be compared across settings. In between blends the two." That matches the code: shown = (1 − s)·t + s·(t − tmin)/(tmax − tmin), with tmin/tmax taken separately for the 47 main-map units and the 23 wards.
- CSV import (added 2026-11-02, user: "i want to be able to feed csv so porsche can upload their own data"; first shipped as one "DATA" row with a combined 10-column file, then the same morning reworked at the user's request, "put effort on UI and not the feature … an upload button for all 4 … a tab saying upload"): the sidebar card now has two tabs, "Controls" (all the old rows) and "Upload" (`role=tablist`, arrow keys, Home/End, nothing remembered between reloads; the map card is unchanged in both). The Upload tab has a one-line lead ("One file per item, one row per region. Nothing is sent online.") and five blocks in `.crow` style: Can park, Can charge, Renewal soon, Can pay, Households. Each block: label + status chip on the right ("placeholder" / "real" with the source in the title / "uploaded · N rows ×" in accent, the × clears that item only), a one-line description, and two raised buttons "Template" and "Upload" (hidden per-item `<input type=file accept=".csv,text/csv">`). Errors ("no park column found", "not a CSV file", "no id or name column found", "no known regions (N skipped)") appear as one accent line under that item's buttons and leave the data unchanged; only one error shows at a time. "Clear all uploads" link at the bottom of the tab when anything is uploaded. Drag-and-drop anywhere on the page still works: a thin accent outline inside the sidebar card while dragging, the file is routed by its columns to every matching item, and the Upload tab opens. When the Upload tab is open the sidebar is taller than the map card, so the footer line sits lower on that tab. Everything runs in the browser (FileReader → TextDecoder utf-8 fatal, falling back to shift_jis for Excel-on-Windows files; BOM stripped; CRLF ok; quoted fields; tab-separated accepted); nothing is sent or stored anywhere, a reload forgets it. Templates are per item: `heatmap-park.csv` / `-charge.csv` / `-timing.csv` with header `id,name_en,name_ja,<item>` (percent, 1 decimal), `heatmap-pay.csv` with `id,name_en,name_ja,money_10m,money_15m,money_20m` (percent, 3 decimals because the over-¥20m shares are tiny and 1 decimal drifted totals by 0.3–0.4%; with 3 the round trip is within 0.03%), `heatmap-households.csv` with `id,name_en,name_ja,households` (integers); all UTF-8 with BOM, CRLF, 70 rows in page order, values = the current base (uploaded values if uploaded). An item's Upload reads only that item's column(s); other columns are ignored and listed in the chip's hover title. The old combined `heatmap-data.csv` is gone. Import: region key column = id / code / region / prefecture / pref / name / name_en / name_ja / 都道府県 / 地域, matched by id, prefecture number 1–47, English name or Japanese name (with or without 都道府県 / 区); value columns park, charge, timing (aliases renewal, buyingsoon…), households (世帯数), money_10m/15m/20m (m10…, over10m…) and plain `money` (= the current cutoff, said in the status). Per column, any value > 1 means percent. Blank cells leave the value alone; bad cells are counted and skipped. A whole-Tokyo row (JP-13 / 13 / Tokyo / 東京都) sets the same share on all 24 Tokyo pieces, and for `households` is split across the 24 pieces in proportion to the built-in counts (remainder on JP-13-REST); ward / JP-13-REST rows in the same file override their piece afterwards. Each item keeps its own backup of the built-in numbers: re-uploading an item replaces only that item, × restores only that item, "Clear all uploads" restores everything. After apply or Clear, `refreshDerived()` recomputes NAT_HH, the per-unit households, the Tokyo sum and OWN_DEFAULT, clears typed overrides and the URL params, and re-renders. Tooltip tags: " · your number" beats " · uploaded" beats " · placeholder" / " · estimated". Errors ("not a CSV file", "no id or name column", "no usable columns", "no known regions (N skipped)") show in the accent colour and leave the data unchanged. With no file loaded the page is identical to before (all 38 harness keys and the blink test unchanged).

## Styling
- The page is light mode only, with a pure white page background (`--bg: #FFFFFF`) and white cards; the cards are separated from the page by the rim ring, not by a tinted background. All colors are tokens on `:root`, and there is no amber or orange anywhere. The accent is navy `#12256E`; the map ramp is white → `#61BCF6` → `#12256E` (see above).
- The toggles use the official iOS light-mode colours: `--switch-on: #34C759` (green) and `--switch-off: #E9E9EA`, with no hover state. Nothing else on the page is green.
- The "Top 8" ranking bars: pill-shaped (`border-radius: 999px` on track and fill) with a very subtle gradient `--bar-from: #3D78B9` → `--accent` (#12256E). The gradient is anchored to the full track (`.rank .bar` is a size container, the fill's `background-size` is `100cqw`), so a growing bar uncovers more of it instead of squeezing a whole gradient into its own width. Short bars look a touch lighter; the longest ends in pure navy.
- Corners are square everywhere except the iOS toggles and the ranking bars, which are pill-shaped.
- Cards, the active segmented button and the inset frame use the "machined rim" recipe: tokens `--groove`, `--rim-top`, `--rim-side`, `--rim-sheen` and `--rim-shadow`.
